# Each scan budget is shared by the queues of its kind in one claim round; a round that uses it up returns what it has

| | |

A claim round has two scan budgets (G-CLM-06): `claim_candidate_budget` caps the "next tenant" probes of all its `round_robin` queues together, and `claim_scan_rows_max` caps the rows read from all its `fifo` queues together. A probe counts whether its tenant is skipped or not. When one kind's budget is spent, the walk stops for queues of that kind and continues with queues of the other kind. A round that takes nothing returns what it has (`429` if it skipped a task for capacity), and the next round starts at the next queue. One `round_robin` queue full of saturated tenants can therefore cost a round its whole probe budget, but not every round.

## Setup
- Engine config (startup): `gears.task_engine.config.claim_candidate_budget: 2`. One engine node.
- **reports** (`round_robin`, `per_tenant_concurrency_limit: 5`): `{tenant_a}`, `{tenant_a1}` and `{tenant_b}` each have 5 tasks `claimed` (by other workers) and 3 more `queued`. Every tenant with queued work in **reports** is at its limit.
- **default** (`round_robin`, unlimited concurrency): `{tenant_b}` has 4 tasks `queued` and none claimed.
- **bulk** (`fifo`): `{tenant_b}` has 4 tasks `queued` and none claimed (used in Exchange 5).
- `{platform_worker}` claims sequentially with `timeout_secs: 0`. Clock `T0`.

## Exchanges 1–4 — the same claim four times, two `round_robin` queues
```http
POST /api/task_engine/v1/tasks/claim HTTP/1.1
Host: api.example.com
Authorization: Bearer <platform-worker-token>
Content-Type: application/json

{ "worker_id": "platform-ops-1", "session_id": "{session_1}",
  "queues": ["gts.cf.core.tasks.queue.v1~acme.reports._.render.v1",
             "gts.cf.core.tasks.queue.v1~cf.core.tasks.default.v1"],
  "max_tasks": 1, "timeout_secs": 0 }
```

## Expected response
- The four answers alternate between `429` and `200`: exactly two of each, never two `200`s or two `429`s in a row (the start queue advances by one each round, G-CLM-04).
- A round that starts at **reports** spends both probes on saturated tenants and returns `429 Too Many Requests` with `Retry-After: 1` (`resource_exhausted`, subject `concurrency`: nothing assigned, a task skipped for capacity). It does not go on to **default**, because **default** is a `round_robin` queue and the shared probe budget is spent.
- A round that starts at **default** returns `200 OK` with one `{tenant_b}` task from **default**.

```http
HTTP/1.1 429 Too Many Requests
Retry-After: 1
Content-Type: application/problem+json
```

## Exchange 5 — a `fifo` queue has its own budget
From the Setup state, the same claim four times with `"queues": [reports, bulk]`:
- Every answer is `200 OK` with one `{tenant_b}` task from **bulk**. A round that starts at **reports** spends the probe budget there, then continues with **bulk**, whose walk uses `claim_scan_rows_max` (G-CLM-06).

## Side effects
- After Exchanges 1–4: two **default** tasks of `{tenant_b}` are `claimed`; nothing in **reports** changes. After Exchange 5: four **bulk** tasks are `claimed`.
- metric `te_claim_capacity_skipped_total{queue="…reports.render.v1", dimension="queue_tenant"}` increases.
- No `429` or `204` round opens or renews a session (G-CLM-16).

## Notes
- With per-queue budgets (DESIGN.md:2447 as written) every claim in Exchanges 1–4 would reach **default** and all four would be `200`; the flow tells the two rules apart without knowing which queue the first round starts at.
- Three saturated tenants against a budget of 2 keep the budget, not the end of the tenant cycle, as the reason the round stops.
- The tenant cursor moves past every tenant a round probes and keeps its place for the next round on that queue (G-CLM-06). So the next round that starts at **reports** probes the third saturated tenant first, not the first one again: running out of budget pauses the walk, and no tenant is skipped for good. The cursor is in node memory per waiter group (DESIGN.md:2513), so this holds on one node.
- A long-poll claim (`timeout_secs > 0`) runs one round per signal or watcher tick, each with fresh budgets and starting at the next queue; a round that takes nothing parks until the next signal or tick (G-CLM-06). The `429` and its `Retry-After` value are the same for a long-poll claim (G-CLM-09).
